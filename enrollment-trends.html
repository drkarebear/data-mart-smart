Why it matters

First Define What “Enrollment” Means

Before you build a trend, decide whether your question is about distinct students or course enrollments. These are not the same measure.

Choose the Measure First

Use Annual/Term Student Count for distinct students. Use a course/enrollment report when the question is about course enrollments or activity within a discipline.

Do this

Build a Clean Comparison

  1. Choose one measure

    Do not switch between Student Count, Enrollment Count, and FTES across years.

  2. Use consistent intervals

    Fall-to-Fall is often cleaner than comparing one Fall term with a full academic year.

  3. Keep the population stable

    Use the same college, discipline, TOP code, course list, student group, or other filters for every period.

  4. Check for course or classification changes

    Course renumbering, prefix changes, common course numbering, curriculum changes, or TOP-code changes can create a false appearance of growth or decline.

  5. Use several periods

    One unusual year is not a trend. Look across enough periods to see whether the pattern persists.

  6. Calculate changes transparently

    Show both the raw difference and the percentage change when useful.

Example Calculation

If a department reported 115 sections in Fall 2022 and 92 in Fall 2026:

Raw change
92 − 115 = −23 sections
Percentage change
−23 ÷ 115 × 100 = −20.0%

The defensible statement is descriptive: reported section count declined 20.0% between the two selected Fall terms. The trend alone does not explain why.

A Trend Does Not Explain the Cause

A decline could reflect scheduling, demand, staffing, curriculum changes, local policy, demographic shifts, modality, competition among courses, or many other factors. Data Mart can identify a pattern worth investigating; additional evidence is usually needed to explain it.
