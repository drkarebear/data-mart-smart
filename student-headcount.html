Data recipe: distinct students

Find Student Headcount

Use this recipe when your question is about how many distinct students attended a college, district, or the system during a term or academic year.

Why Might I Use This?

  • Program review
  • Enrollment planning
  • Equity inquiry
  • College context

What You Are Trying to Measure

You are counting distinct students who meet the report's inclusion rules—not course enrollments.

Student Count Is Not Enrollment Count

One student can enroll in several courses. A headcount of people and a count of course enrollments answer different questions.

Best Data Mart Report

Annual/Term Student Count is the best starting report for broad distinct-student headcount questions.

Open the official Annual/Term Student Count report

How to Set It Up

  1. Choose the geography

    Select statewide, districtwide, collegewide, or the specific district/college you need.

  2. Choose Term Search or Annual Search

    Use a term when you want one term; use annual when the question is about the academic year.

  3. Select the time period

    Use the report's live selector. “Latest available” can change as districts submit data.

  4. View the report

    Start simple. Add only the dimensions needed for the question.

  5. Add demographic or headcount-status fields only when useful

    Examples can include College Name, Gender, Age Group, Ethnicity, or Headcount Status.

  6. Read Notes & Links before interpreting

    Current report notes matter because the counting logic changed in January 2026.

Important 2026 Headcount Change

Do Not Add College Headcounts to Rebuild the District Total

The current report uses distinct student counts at the statewide, districtwide, and collegewide levels. Statewide and districtwide totals are deduplicated. A student attending more than one college can appear in multiple college counts but only once in a deduplicated district total.

Annual Counts Are Not the Sum of Term Counts

Annual student counts are deduplicated across terms. A student who attends in Fall and Spring should not be counted twice in the annual headcount simply because they appear in both term counts.

What the Result Will Mean

A result such as 8,500 students means 8,500 distinct students meeting the report's selected geography, period, headcount-status, and other filters. It does not mean 8,500 course enrollments.
