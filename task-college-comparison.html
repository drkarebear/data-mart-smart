Peer comparison

I Need to Compare My College With Other Colleges

Choose peers for a reason, use the same measure everywhere, and describe the difference before trying to explain it.

Choose the source by the comparison

DataVista

Often the best first source for defined student outcomes, equity, pathways, and broad program metrics that you want to compare consistently across colleges.

Data Mart

Useful when the comparison is specifically a detailed public MIS report such as Grade Distribution, Course Details, sections, awards, or another traditional query.

Local context

Needed to understand differences in college size, mission, curriculum, modality, staffing, admissions constraints, schedule design, facilities, and regional context.

Before you compare two numbers

  • Same source and metric? Similar labels can hide different calculations.
  • Same timeframe? Compare term with term or academic year with academic year.
  • Same population and program definition? Keep student group, course/TOP, award type, and other filters consistent.
  • Same numerator and denominator? This is essential for rates.
  • Comparable institutions? Nearby is not automatically comparable.

A rank is not an explanation

Peer comparisons should generate inquiry, not label a college or program as better or worse. Program size, mission, modality, student population, and local structures can all shape the observed difference.

Better wording

Descriptive: “College A reported the highest value for this defined metric among the selected colleges.”

Too strong without more evidence: “College A has the strongest program.”
