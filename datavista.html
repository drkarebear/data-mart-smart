DataVista: When It Is Worth Using

DataVista is strongest for cohorts, equity, pathways, completion, transfer, labor-market outcomes, and other questions that benefit from a connected student-outcomes framework. For a narrow term, course, grade, count, or report question, Data Mart is often faster and simpler.

Open official DataVistaCompare DataVista and Data Mart

Use DataVista when your question sounds like this

  • What happened to the same starting group of students over time?
  • Where are the equity gaps using current statewide methods?
  • How are students progressing through pathways or first-year milestones?
  • What are the completion or transfer outcomes?
  • What are the labor-market or post-college outcomes?
  • What does a current statewide initiative report show?
  • How does one annual metric look across years, programs, or available disaggregations?

Do not use DataVista just because it is newer

If your question is simply “How many sections were offered in Fall 2025?”, “What grades were awarded?”, or “What was the term success rate?”, Data Mart is usually the more direct starting point.

Performance tip

DataVista can be powerful and slow at the same time.

The official DataVista release webinar notes that selecting All Students can take longer because it loads a large amount of data. The platform is doing more than a simple report query, so use the smallest view that fits the question.

  • One metric? Start in Single Metric.
  • One student group? Select it instead of a broader population when possible.
  • Simple term/course/grade question? Use Data Mart.
  • Large multi-year analysis? Consider a DataVista data download instead of repeatedly loading broad dashboard views.

DataVista's modern cloud architecture speeds the back-end processing pipeline, but that is different from every interactive view loading instantly.

Before you quote a DataVista number

  1. Student group: Who is actually included?
  2. Snapshot or cohort: Is this a selected-year population or the same starting group followed over time?
  3. Denominator: What population is the percentage based on?
  4. Completeness: Could a recent cohort outcome still update?
  5. Suppression: Is a small group masked?

Calculated FTES is not official apportionment FTES

Use DataVista calculated FTES only for the purpose supported by its metric definition. For official apportionment or CCFS-320 questions, use the appropriate official/local fiscal source.
