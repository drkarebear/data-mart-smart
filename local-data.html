When Local Data Comes First

Statewide systems are powerful for context and comparison. They are not live registration, scheduling, staffing, or budget systems.

Start locally when the question sounds like this

Should we add a section?

Look at current enrollment, waitlists, fill rate, cancellations, time of day, modality, and the live schedule.

Do we have enough faculty?

Look at current FTEF, assignment data, full-time/adjunct load, overload, vacancies, and the schedule that must be staffed.

Which class is filling right now?

Use the local SIS, Power BI, or institutional dashboard that reflects current registration activity.

Why is this local number different?

Check the local definition, refresh date, exclusions, census timing, and whether the statewide metric uses a different population or calculation.

Then choose the statewide source by question

  1. Local data: What is happening now?
  2. Data Mart: Do I need a specific term, course, grade, section, award, service, or staffing report?
  3. DataVista: Do I need a cohort, equity, pathway, completion, transfer, labor-market, or integrated annual outcome?

Program review is usually a multi-source job

Use the simplest valid source for each question. Do not force the whole review into one statewide platform.

When local and statewide numbers disagree

Do not average them or choose the one you like better. Document the source, refresh date, population, time period, and definition for each. A difference may be completely legitimate because the systems are answering different questions.
