💡 Data Mart Smart Tips

These are the practical moves that keep a useful number from becoming a misleading one.

Start With These Eight

You do not need to be an institutional researcher to use data thoughtfully.

Students Are Not Enrollments

One student can generate several course enrollments. Decide whether your question is about people or course-taking activity before choosing a measure.

Example: One student taking four courses may count as one student but four enrollments.

Sections Are Not Always Classes

A reported section record may be part of a linked lecture/lab, support, co-requisite, or other local structure.

Ask: Am I counting reported section records or trying to estimate distinct instructional classes?

Know Your Denominator

A percentage is incomplete without the population underneath it. Compare both the rate and the underlying N.

Example: 90% of 20 enrollments and 90% of 2,000 enrollments deserve different context.

Compare Like With Like

Use the same report, measure, time period, population, and definitions before interpreting differences across colleges or years.

Check: same measure + same period + same population + same definition.

Do Not Double Count

Totals, subtotals, overlapping categories, and district totals can quietly inflate a result.

Example: Do not add a district total to the individual colleges that already make up that district total.

TOP Code ≠ Department Prefix

A TOP code classifies an instructional area. A local prefix is a college's course identifier. They can overlap, but they are not the same thing.

Example: “English” could mean ENGL courses, ENGLISH courses, or an English-related TOP code.

A Difference Is Not a Cause

Data Mart can show that two colleges or years differ. It usually cannot establish why the difference occurred.

Prefer: “College A reported more awards.” Avoid: “College A has a stronger program.”

Blank Does Not Automatically Mean Zero

A blank cell could be unavailable, suppressed, not reported, or something else. Do not turn it into zero unless the documentation says that is correct.

Rule: Name missingness accurately before calculating.

One Year Is Not a Trend

A single term can be unusual. Use consistent intervals over several periods before declaring growth or decline.

Better: Fall-to-Fall for several years rather than Fall 2025 versus the full 2025–26 academic year.

Save Your Method

Record the report, filters, measure, time period, institutions, and retrieval date. If special handling was needed, document that too.

Goal: Another person should be able to reproduce your result.

The Bigger Idea

Data can tell you that something is happening. They do not always tell you why. The strongest use of Data Mart often combines quantitative patterns with local knowledge, student experience, curriculum context, and other evidence.

Found Something Interesting?

Finding the number is not the end of the work. Use a simple inquiry process to describe the pattern, check what the data support, add local context, and decide what to ask next.
