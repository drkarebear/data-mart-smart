Understand the Number Before You Interpret It

These are the distinctions most likely to change what a CCC data number means.

Start with these

You do not need to be an institutional researcher to ask careful data questions.

Students Are Not Enrollments

One person can generate several course enrollments. Decide whether the question is about people or course-taking activity.

Snapshot Is Not Cohort

Snapshot asks what happened within a selected year. Cohort follows the same defined starting group over time.

See the full distinction

Suppressed Is Not Zero

DataVista generally masks small counts and can suppress an additional group so the hidden value cannot be reconstructed.

Incomplete Is Not Final

Some newer cohort outcomes can change as additional years of data become available. DataVista marks incomplete years visually.

Know Your Denominator

A percentage is incomplete without the population underneath it. Compare both the rate and the underlying N when available.

Sections Are Not Always Classes

A reported section record can be part of a linked lecture/lab, support, co-requisite, or another local structure.

FTES Depends on the Method

Do not assume every FTES field is official apportionment FTES. DataVista explicitly labels its program-level FTES as calculated and not the CCFS-320 methodology.

TOP Code Is Not a Local Prefix

A TOP code classifies an instructional area. A local subject prefix identifies courses at a college. They overlap but are not interchangeable.

Compare Like With Like

Use the same source, metric, population, timeframe, and definitions before interpreting differences across colleges or years.

A Difference Is Not a Cause

A statewide pattern can tell you that groups or years differ. It usually cannot establish why.

One Year Is Not a Trend

Use comparable intervals across several periods before declaring growth or decline.

Save Your Method

Record the source, report/metric, population, period, denominator, filters, locale, retrieval date, and any special handling.

The bigger idea

Data can tell you that something is happening. They do not always tell you why. The strongest institutional inquiry combines quantitative patterns with local knowledge, student experience, curriculum context, and other evidence.
