From finding a number to using it well

From Data to a Conversation

You found something interesting. The next step is not to jump to a conclusion. It is to turn the result into a better institutional question.

Data Are for Inquiry, Not a Scorecard

A higher or lower number is not automatically evidence of a better or worse program, instructor, department, or college. Data Mart is most useful for identifying patterns worth understanding. Professional judgment, local context, student experience, and other evidence may be needed to understand why a pattern exists.

A Six-Step Inquiry Process

  1. Describe what you see without explaining it

    Start with the observation the data actually support.

    Better: “Communication Studies award count increased from X to Y between the selected academic years.”

    Too soon: “Our curriculum change caused more students to graduate.”

  2. Check the measure and comparison

    Confirm that you used the same report, population, time interval, classification, and denominator across the comparison.

  3. Ask what the data cannot tell you

    Data Mart may show that a difference or trend exists. It often cannot establish the cause.

  4. Bring in practitioner and student context

    Faculty, classified professionals, counselors, administrators, and students may know about curriculum, scheduling, staffing, service access, policy changes, or lived experience that helps explain the pattern.

  5. Identify the next question

    Decide what you need to investigate next rather than trying to make one report answer everything.

  6. Choose the right source for that next question

    The next answer may come from Data Mart, your local institutional research office, scheduling or curriculum data, surveys, focus groups, student feedback, or several sources together.

A Department Meeting Discussion Template

  • What do we notice?
  • What surprises us?
  • What can this measure tell us?
  • What can it not tell us?
  • What local context might matter?
  • Whose perspective is missing?
  • What do we want to investigate next?

When to Bring in Institutional Research

Data Mart is useful for many reproducible public-data questions. Local institutional research or other campus experts become especially important when you need student-level longitudinal analysis, locally defined populations, causal analysis, data not reported to the Chancellor's Office, confidential data, or a high-stakes institutional conclusion.

Not Sure What to Ask Next?

CCC Data Mart Coach can help you separate what your current result supports from what would require another source or another question.
