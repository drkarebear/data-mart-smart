Purpose pathway: identify gaps, then investigate

Use Data for Equity Inquiry

Use disaggregated evidence to identify differences in access, participation, services, outcomes, and completion that warrant further inquiry.

What Data Can Help You Do

Equity analysis begins by describing disparities accurately. The next work is to understand structures, experiences, policies, and conditions that may contribute to those disparities.

Useful Data Mart Starting Points

  • student demographic breakouts
  • success and retention outcomes
  • grade distribution
  • program awards
  • student-services participation
  • transfer and enrollment patterns

What to Pair With Data Mart

  • local disproportionate-impact analysis
  • student voice and qualitative research
  • campus climate and service-access information
  • course and program context
  • community and regional context

Questions to Bring to the Conversation

  • Which group differences are large enough to investigate?
  • Are the denominators and populations comparable?
  • Does the pattern persist over time?
  • What barriers might students experience that the data cannot show?
  • Who should be included in interpreting the result?

Important Boundary

A disparity is not a diagnosis. Avoid attributing differences to students or communities without evidence, and do not infer cause from a descriptive gap alone.
