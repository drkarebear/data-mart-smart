One distinction changes the whole question

Snapshot or Cohort?

Both views are useful. They describe different populations and different kinds of evidence.

Think: a photograph

Snapshot View

A snapshot view displays information for students who meet the criteria for each metric within the selected year. The students represented in one metric do not have to be the exact same students represented in another metric.

Good snapshot questions

  • How many sections were offered in this program area this year?
  • How many enrollments were reported this year?
  • What proportion of the defined student group met this outcome in the selected year?

Do not silently turn a snapshot into a pathway

If one metric shows transfer-level English completion and another shows degree completion in the same year, do not assume those outcomes describe the same students moving from one step to the next.

Think: following a group down a path

Cohort View

A cohort view follows the same defined group of students over time and asks whether that group reaches an outcome within a selected timeframe. DataVista cohort timeframes can include one, two, three, four, or six years depending on the student group and metric.

Good cohort questions

  • What happened to students who first entered the CCC system in this cohort year?
  • What proportion completed a milestone within two years?
  • How do outcomes differ across student subgroups for the same starting cohort?

A newer cohort year may not be finished yet

Some cohort metrics need additional years of source data before every student has had the full amount of time allowed to meet the outcome. DataVista identifies incomplete years visually, including lighter bars or open data points.

Incomplete does not mean wrong

It means the metric can still change as additional data are incorporated. Before describing a recent cohort as a final outcome, check whether the selected year and timeframe are marked complete.

Fast decision rule

Use Snapshot when...

Your question begins with “What happened in this year?”

Use Cohort when...

Your question begins with “What happened to the students who started together?”
