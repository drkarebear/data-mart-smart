Purpose pathway: progression, completion, and barriers

Use Data for Student Success and Pathways

Use outcomes and pathway-related evidence to identify where students may be progressing, stopping, completing, or encountering possible structural barriers.

If You Only Have 2 Minutes

  • Use Data Mart to describe a pattern, scale, or comparison—not to prove a cause.
  • Pair public historical data with the current local evidence that matters for the decision.
  • End with the next question or action your team needs to take.

What it means

What Data Can Help You Do

Data can help surface questions about course outcomes, program completions, transfer, and the structure of pathways. Faculty contextual knowledge is especially important because the same pattern can have multiple explanations.

Do this

Useful Data Mart Starting Points

  • Enrollment Retention and Success Rate
  • Grade Distribution
  • Program Awards
  • Transfer reports
  • course and section reports
  • student headcount and enrollment trends

Why it matters

What to Pair With Data Mart

  • local sequence and cohort data
  • student education plans
  • time-to-completion and excess-unit information
  • student interviews or surveys
  • curriculum maps and prerequisite structures

What next

Questions to Bring to the Conversation

  • Where do students appear to lose momentum?
  • Are course availability or sequencing constraints visible?
  • Do outcomes differ by student group?
  • Are students accumulating more units than the pathway requires?
  • What can faculty change, and what requires institutional action?

Watch for

Important Boundary

No single Data Mart report represents a complete student pathway. Use state reporting to surface questions and pair it with local longitudinal and student-level evidence when pathway progression is the issue.

Turn the Purpose Into a Query

If you know the decision or conversation but not the report, use Find the Right Report or CCC Data Mart Coach.

Guided Pathways Lens

Faculty can use data to investigate possible unclear pathways, excess units, scheduling barriers, program visibility, completion patterns, and related program-review questions. The data identify places to look; faculty and students help explain what the numbers mean in practice.
