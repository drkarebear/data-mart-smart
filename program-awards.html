Why it matters

Decide What Counts as an Award for Your Question

The report supports different award categories. Be explicit about whether you want associate degrees only, certificates, all awards, or another defined subset.

All Awards vs. Chancellor's Office Approved Awards

The report includes different award-selection modes. Do not assume they are interchangeable. Use the live report notes and official award-code definitions when a category is unclear.

Do this

How to Set It Up

  1. Choose geography

    Select the colleges, district, or statewide view that matches the question.

  2. Select the academic year

    Use the same year or set of years for every institution in the comparison.

  3. Choose the award-selection mode

    Decide whether your question calls for All Awards or Chancellor's Office Approved Awards.

  4. Select the program/TOP area

    Use the same instructional classification across the comparison.

  5. View the report and add useful fields

    College Name, Award Type, CDCP status, and the needed TOP level can help define the result.

  6. Do not combine award categories until you know they can be combined

    A person can be associated with more than one award record.

What it means

What Award Count Means

Award Count is a count of reported awards. It is not automatically a count of unique graduates. One person can earn more than one award.

Do Not Turn Raw Awards Into Program Quality

A college reporting more awards does not by itself establish a stronger program. Program size, enrollment, award structures, curriculum, student pathways, and other factors can affect the count.
