Task guide: resource allocation

I Need Evidence for a Resource Request

Connect the requested resource to a documented student or program need, show the scale or persistence of that need, and identify what outcome the investment is intended to improve.

In a Hurry?

  1. State the need before choosing a metric.
  2. Choose Data Mart measures that actually describe that need.
  3. Use a trend rather than a single convenient year when possible.
  4. Add local evidence about workload, capacity, cost, access, or service gaps.
  5. Explain what the resource would change and how you would know whether it helped.

Evidence to Pull From Data Mart

  • student headcount and enrollment trends
  • sections and FTES
  • program awards
  • success/retention where relevant
  • student-services participation
  • faculty/staff summaries for broad context

Evidence to Add From Your College

  • budget and cost information
  • staffing/workload and service capacity
  • facilities and technology limitations
  • student feedback or unmet-demand evidence
  • program review priorities
  • college resource-allocation criteria and strategic goals

Questions the Data Should Help You Ask

  • What problem would this resource solve?
  • Who is affected and how do we know?
  • Is the need persistent?
  • What evidence is missing from Data Mart?
  • What measurable change should follow the investment?

Do Not Let One Number Make the Decision

Resource allocation is a governance and planning decision. A large program or unfavorable comparison does not automatically establish priority. Evidence should clarify need, not manufacture inevitability.

When Data Mart Is Not Enough

Bring in your institutional research office or another appropriate local expert when the question requires student-level longitudinal analysis, a locally defined cohort, confidential information, live operational data, causal analysis, or a high-stakes institutional conclusion.

Method Language You Can Adapt

I selected Data Mart measures that directly relate to the stated need and reviewed them across comparable periods. I paired those public data with local capacity, workload, cost, student-need, and planning evidence before making the resource request.
