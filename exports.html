1. Identify the Structure

Before touching a formula, identify the report, title or query metadata, time period, filters, rows, columns, measures, totals, subtotals, and blank or suppressed cells.

2. Reconstruct the Question

State what the export appears to be answering. If you cannot explain the unit of analysis and measure in one or two sentences, the spreadsheet is not ready for interpretation.

3. Inspect Before Calculating

  • Are district totals mixed with college rows?
  • Are statewide totals present?
  • Are subtotals repeated?
  • Do categories overlap?
  • Are there multiple measures in the same table?
  • Do course or section rows repeat?
  • Could lecture/lab or linked components be represented separately?
  • Are blank cells actually zero?

Do Not Automatically Sum a Column

A spreadsheet can contain perfectly valid numbers that are not valid to add together. Confirm that the categories are mutually exclusive and that totals or subtotals are not already included.

4. Calculate Only After the Structure Is Clear

Once the categories are valid to combine, calculate differences, percentage changes, shares, averages, or ratios transparently enough that another person can reproduce the work.

5. Explain the Result in Plain Language

Name the measure, population, time period, grouping, and any caveat that affects interpretation.
