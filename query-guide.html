Four question types cover most faculty data work

Specific and report-based

“How many / which / what grades / what term?”

Use Data Mart first. This includes student counts, course details, sections, enrollments, grade distribution, term success/retention, awards, services, and faculty/staff reports.

See Data Mart question examples

Longitudinal and outcome-based

“What happened over time / to the same students / across groups?”

Use DataVista first. This includes cohorts, equity, pathways, completion, transfer, labor-market outcomes, and statewide initiative metrics.

See DataVista question examples

Current and operational

“What is happening right now?”

Use local data first. This includes current enrollment, waitlists, fill, scheduling, staffing, FTEF, budget, and institution-specific definitions.

See local-data examples

Use Data Mart for specific term, course, and count questions

Students

“How many distinct students did the college serve in Fall 2025?”

Open the official Annual/Term Student Count report

Courses

“Which English courses were reported in Fall 2025?”

Open the official Course Details report

Sections and enrollments

“How many sections and enrollments were reported for this TOP code?”

Open the official Credit Courses/Sections report

Grades and course outcomes

“What grades were awarded?” or “What was the Fall success rate?”

Open Grade Distribution · Open Retention/Success Rate

Use DataVista when the question needs a student journey or integrated outcomes

One annual metric

“How has this success, completion, or transfer metric changed over time?”

Several related measures

“What is happening across progress, success, employment, or earnings?”

Open DataVista Metric Themes

“Where are the equity gaps?” or “What are the Guided Pathways / Vision 2030 / SWP metrics?”

Use local data when the question depends on now

Examples: “Is ENGL C1000 full today?” “Which sections have waitlists?” “What is our current FTEF?” “Should we add a late-start section?” “Which faculty are assigned?”

Open the Local Data guide

If the question is “What should we do?” use more than one source

Program review, accreditation, staffing, resource requests, and schedule planning are decisions, not just data lookups. Combine the most relevant statewide evidence with current local context.
