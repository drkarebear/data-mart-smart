Find the Right Data Mart Report

You do not need to memorize the Data Mart menu. Start with what you want to know, decide what needs to be counted, and then choose the report that matches the question.

The report is not the research question. The report is the tool you choose after the question is clear.

Before You Choose a Report

Four quick decisions prevent a lot of confusion later.

1

What do you want to count?

Students, enrollments, sections, FTES, grades, awards, or something else?

2

Who or what belongs in the population?

A college, district, discipline, TOP code, course, program, or student group?

3

What time period matches the question?

A term, academic year, fiscal year, or multi-year trend?

4

What comparison do you need?

Across colleges, across years, across student groups, or no comparison at all?

Choose the Question Closest to Yours

These are common starting points for faculty, chairs, program leads, and college staff.

How many students are enrolled?

Use this path when your unit of analysis is the student rather than the individual course enrollment.

Best starting match

Annual/Term Student Count

  • Student headcount by term or annual period
  • Useful for demographic breakouts and broad enrollment questions
  • Annual counts should not be created by simply adding term counts

How many sections are being offered?

The right report depends on whether you need identifiable courses or a broader aggregate.

Specific courses

Course Details

  • Useful for Course ID, title, control number, TOP code, units, and Sections Count
  • Best when you need to inspect particular courses
  • Review linked or related section structures before treating every section record as an independent class
Open Course Details See the section guide →

How many total credit sections, enrollments, or FTES?

Use an aggregate course report when the question is about totals by college or classification rather than individual courses.

Broader aggregate

Credit Courses/Sections

  • Counts credit sections, enrollment, and FTES
  • Useful for comparisons by college and course characteristics
  • Do not assume its section results are interchangeable with Course Details

How are students doing in courses?

Use this path for success and retention rates rather than raw enrollment alone.

Best starting match

Enrollment Retention and Success Rate

  • Compares enrollment, retention, and success measures

  • Keep numerator, denominator, population, and time period consistent across comparisons

Data Caution: Similar-Sounding Measures Can Answer Different Questions

Student Count is not Enrollment Count. A section is not a course. An award is not automatically a unique graduate. A percentage is not meaningful without its denominator. Define the measure before you compare the number.
