Enrollment planning

Use Data for Enrollment Planning and Scheduling

When the decision is about the next schedule, start with the most current local data. Statewide sources add context, not live demand.

1. Start with local operational data

  • current enrollment and seat capacity;
  • waitlists and unmet demand;
  • fill rates and cancellations;
  • time of day, location, modality, and late-start patterns;
  • course sequencing and prerequisite bottlenecks;
  • current faculty availability and assignment constraints.

2. Add DataVista for multi-year context

Use program-level sections, enrollments, online/DE sections, calculated FTES, and other relevant measures to see whether the current pattern fits or breaks from the broader trajectory.

3. Use Data Mart when the question needs a specific report

Course Details, Credit Courses/Sections, or other term-level public reports can add detail when the local and DataVista views do not answer the question by themselves.

Do not schedule from lagged statewide data alone

A statewide report can show historical demand or context, but it cannot tell you whether a section is filling this morning, whether a waitlist has formed, or whether a current time slot is working.

A strong scheduling statement sounds like this

“Current local data show sustained waitlist demand for the evening section, while the three-year statewide trend shows stable or growing program activity. We recommend adding one evening section and reviewing fill and success after census.”
