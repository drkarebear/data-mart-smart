Best Data Mart Report

Grade Distribution is the best starting report when the composition of reported grades is the focus.

Open the official Grade Distribution report

Use This Report When

  • you want counts of specific reported grades;
  • you want to compare grade patterns across colleges or instructional areas;
  • you need more detail than one overall success percentage;
  • you want to examine the composition of outcomes before deciding what summary measure is appropriate.

How to Set It Up

  1. Choose geography

    Select the college, district, or statewide view that matches the question.

  2. Select the term

    Use the same term when comparing institutions or groups.

  3. View the report

    Start with the simplest layout that answers the question.

  4. Add needed dimensions

    Useful fields can include district/college, credit status, and two-, four-, or six-digit TOP.

  5. Inspect the grade categories

    Do not silently combine categories until you know the grouping is methodologically appropriate.

Grade Distribution vs. Success/Retention

Choose the Report That Matches the Question

Use Grade Distribution when you need grade composition. Use Enrollment Retention and Success Rate when you need the Chancellor's Office success/retention formulas.

What the Result Means

The output is a distribution of reported grades for the selected enrollment population. It does not by itself explain why a pattern exists or whether a college's grading practices are “better” or “worse.”

You Found a Result. What Should You Ask Next?

  • Would the success/retention report answer the summary-rate question better?
  • Are the compared populations equivalent?
  • What additional context would help explain the grade pattern?
