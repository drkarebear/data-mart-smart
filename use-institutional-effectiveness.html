Use Data for Institutional Effectiveness and Accreditation

Use public outcomes and trends as one part of a broader evidence system for institutional reflection, planning, accreditation, and monitoring improvement.

If You Only Have 2 Minutes

  • Use Data Mart to describe a pattern, scale, or comparison,not to prove a cause.
  • Pair public historical data with the current local evidence that matters for the decision.
  • End with the next question or action your team needs to take.

What it means

What Data Can Help You Do

Institutional effectiveness asks whether colleges are achieving their goals and improving student outcomes. Data Mart can provide standardized public context, while accreditation and local planning usually require a wider body of evidence.

Do this

Useful Data Mart Starting Points

  • student headcount and enrollment trends
  • success and retention
  • program awards
  • transfer
  • faculty/staff summaries
  • student-services participation

Why it matters

What to Pair With Data Mart

  • local strategic-plan metrics
  • ACCJC annual and institutional reporting
  • SLO/SAO assessment
  • program review
  • budget and resource-allocation evidence
  • local surveys and qualitative evidence

What next

Questions to Bring to the Conversation

  • What institutional goal does this measure inform?
  • Is the measure stable and defined consistently over time?
  • What local evidence complements the statewide measure?
  • What action followed the last review of this evidence?
  • How is the institution closing the loop?

Watch for

Important Boundary

Do not treat Data Mart as the institution's complete accreditation evidence set. It is one public source among local, qualitative, assessment, planning, and fiscal evidence.
