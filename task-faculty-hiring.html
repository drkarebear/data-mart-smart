Task guide: faculty prioritization

I Need Evidence for a Faculty Hiring Request

Build a defensible picture of instructional demand and program need without pretending that Data Mart alone can determine whether a position should be authorized.

In a Hurry?

  1. Define the discipline/program and use a consistent multi-year period.
  2. Pull sections, enrollments, and FTES to describe instructional activity.
  3. Add student headcount or awards where they genuinely illuminate program scale or outcomes.
  4. Add local full-time/part-time staffing, vacancies, retirements, overload, and scheduling constraints.
  5. Connect the evidence to your college’s actual faculty-prioritization criteria.

Evidence to Pull From Data Mart

  • Course Details for discipline/course-level section patterns
  • Credit Courses/Sections for sections, enrollments, and instructional activity
  • FTES for scale and trend context
  • Student Headcount when distinct students matter
  • Program Awards when the discipline supports a degree/certificate pathway
  • Faculty & Staff reports for broad public staffing context

Evidence to Add From Your College

  • number and percentage of sections taught by full-time and adjunct faculty
  • retirements, vacancies, failed searches, leaves, and reassigned time
  • overload and adjunct assignment patterns
  • waitlists, unstaffed sections, cancellations, and hard-to-cover times
  • department responsibilities such as coordination, curriculum, assessment, mentoring, and governance
  • local faculty-prioritization rubric and budget constraints

Questions the Data Should Help You Ask

  • Is instructional demand persistent across several years?
  • What work is not visible in section counts?
  • Has a retirement or vacancy changed the department’s capacity?
  • Are students losing access because sections cannot be staffed or scheduled?
  • How does the request meet the college’s published prioritization criteria?

Do Not Let One Number Make the Decision

A high section count, FTES figure, adjunct percentage, or peer-college comparison does not by itself prove that a full-time position should be funded. Hiring decisions require local workload, fiscal, governance, program, equity, and institutional-priority evidence.

When Data Mart Is Not Enough

Bring in your institutional research office or another appropriate local expert when the question requires student-level longitudinal analysis, a locally defined cohort, confidential information, live operational data, causal analysis, or a high-stakes institutional conclusion.

Method Language You Can Adapt

I used CCC Data Mart to describe multi-year instructional activity and program scale using consistent definitions. I supplemented those public measures with local staffing, workload, vacancy, scheduling, and program-responsibility evidence because Data Mart does not represent the full scope of faculty need.
