Faculty prioritization

I Need Evidence for a Faculty Hiring Request

Start with the work that must be staffed and the local capacity to do it. Statewide data can strengthen context, but they cannot decide whether a position should be authorized.

1. Start with local staffing reality

  • full-time and adjunct FTEF/load;
  • vacancies, retirements, failed searches, leaves, and reassigned time;
  • overload and adjunct assignment patterns;
  • unstaffed sections, cancellations, waitlists, and hard-to-cover times;
  • curriculum, assessment, mentoring, coordination, and governance work;
  • the college's published faculty-prioritization criteria.

2. Add DataVista for program and student context

Use multi-year sections, enrollments, program activity, student outcomes, equity, and other relevant metrics to show the broader scale and trajectory of the instructional area.

3. Use Data Mart for specific detailed evidence

Course Details, Credit Courses/Sections, traditional FTES reports, Student Headcount, Program Awards, or Faculty & Staff reports may be useful when they answer a specific part of the hiring case.

No one-number hiring formula

A high section count, FTES figure, adjunct percentage, success rate, or peer comparison does not by itself prove that a full-time position should be funded. Hiring decisions require local workload, fiscal, governance, program, equity, and institutional-priority evidence.

Questions the evidence should help answer

  • Is instructional demand persistent?
  • What work is invisible in section counts?
  • Has a vacancy or retirement changed department capacity?
  • Are students losing access because sections cannot be staffed?
  • How does the request meet the college's actual prioritization criteria?

Method language you can adapt

I used local staffing and scheduling evidence to define the capacity problem, then added consistent multi-year statewide measures to describe instructional activity and program context. I did not treat any single statewide metric as a hiring formula.
