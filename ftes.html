What FTES Is and Is Not

FTES is an instructional-activity measure. It is not headcount, Enrollment Count, faculty FTE, or a count of full-time students.

Data Mart FTES Is Not the Same Methodology as CCFS-320 FTES

The Data Mart FTES Summary uses a derived-hours methodology and explicitly warns that it is not the same as the CCFS-320 fiscal/apportionment methodology. Do not substitute one for the other without confirming the purpose.

Best Starting Reports

Use Full-Time Equivalent Students (FTES) for credit, noncredit, and total FTES. Use Distance Education FTES when the question specifically concerns distance education versus non-distance education.

How Data Mart FTES Is Derived

The current Data Mart FTES Summary sums derived Total Hours for eligible enrollment records and divides by 525. Records without derivable Total Hours are excluded.

Conceptual formula
Sum of eligible derived Total Hours ÷ 525

How to Use FTES Carefully

  1. Define why you need FTES

    Use it when instructional activity is the question, not simply because it is available.

  2. Choose the correct FTES report

    Use the general summary or the distance-education version based on the research question.

  3. Keep geography and time periods consistent

    Compare equivalent colleges/periods.

  4. Separate credit and noncredit when relevant

    Do not combine categories unless the question calls for total FTES.

  5. State the methodology when FTES is consequential

    Especially in planning or resource contexts, name that the figure comes from Data Mart rather than a fiscal apportionment report.

A Better Interpretation

What it means

Prefer: “Data Mart reports X FTES for the selected population and period.”

Avoid presenting that number as though it were automatically the official fiscal/apportionment FTES used for every institutional purpose.
