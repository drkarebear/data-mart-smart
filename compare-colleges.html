Make the comparison defensible

Compare Colleges Without Mixing Measures

College comparisons can be useful, but only when the numbers are genuinely comparable. Use this page before ranking, averaging, or interpreting differences.

Before You Compare Two Numbers

  • ✓Same report? Two reports can use similar labels but different populations or counting logic.
  • ✓Same measure? Student Count, Enrollment Count, Sections Count, FTES, Award Count, and rates are not interchangeable.
  • ✓Same time period? Compare term with term or academic year with academic year.
  • ✓Same population? Use the same course, TOP, award type, credit status, or student-group definition.
  • ✓Same numerator and denominator? This matters especially for success, retention, transfer, and other rates.
  • ✓No hidden overlap? Check district totals, subtotals, overlapping categories, and related section records.

Raw Counts Often Need Context

A larger college may naturally report more sections, enrollments, or awards than a smaller college. A raw count can still answer a valid question, but it should not automatically be treated as a measure of program strength.

Better Wording

Descriptive: “College A reported the highest section count among the selected colleges.”

Too strong without more evidence: “College A has the strongest program.”

Useful Context to Add

  • total college enrollment;
  • program or discipline enrollment;
  • FTES;
  • number of awards;
  • multi-year trends;
  • local program structure and scheduling practices.

Do Not Rebuild a Deduplicated Total by Addition

The current Annual/Term Student Count report uses deduplicated distinct counts for statewide and districtwide reporting. Adding college headcounts can therefore produce a different number from the official districtwide count because one student can attend more than one college.
